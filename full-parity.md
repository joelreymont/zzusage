# Full zzusage parity ledger

Pinned original: `zzusage` `9e060d9a767789ab6aaeb8739464bfb70da94b3e`, `src/main.zig` (1,117 lines). It reads local Claude JSONL; it has no HTTP, account API, TLS, or credential path. The full reference in `benchmark/zig-full/main.zig` preserves application behavior with Zig 0.17 API adaptations and two allocator-ownership repairs (see `benchmark/zig-scope.txt`). Its help writer also preserves the original swallowed write error. The expanded full differential passed **100/100** against both stripped ReleaseSmall and the smallest measured Zig configuration: 99 cases compare exact exit, stdout, and stderr bytes, while the concurrent append case checks that each binary retains its initial event despite nondeterministic append counts; see `benchmark/results/full-suite-small.json` and `benchmark/results/minimal-zig/full-suite-zzusage.json`.

Date formatting deliberately follows the source's forward walk from 1970. An adjusted timestamp in the first day before epoch yields `1970-01-00`, and an oversized day late in year 9999 can yield a five-digit year. The port does not replace these source outputs with a conventional calendar normalization. Month 13 also retains the source's arithmetic, but months above 13 are rejected before they could read past the month table. Daily, monthly, and statusline reports skip adjusted timestamps earlier than `-86400` seconds, where the source's negative day cannot safely be converted to `u8`. Sessions continue to use their raw timestamps and ignore timezone for grouping. These guards are applied in both the Habu port and its adapted Zig differential reference; they are deliberate safety divergences from the preserved original Zig code.

The Zig threaded output has no stable single-byte target in contested fixtures: with 24 synthetic files, 40 equivalent daily text runs produced 40 distinct outputs, and 40 sessions JSON runs selected two different first-project labels. The harness therefore does **not** normalize ordinary deterministic text/set order, JSON order, or malformed JSON. It excludes only fixtures whose original output was empirically variable. Habu is serial, so it cannot reproduce every worker scheduling choice of one Zig run.

The focused-review fixes preserve signed/underscore integer timestamp slices,
five-digit monthly keys, and incremental hash-map rehash order for session ties.
Habu also records the capacity reached by every map put, including duplicates,
so the final rehash and iteration order match the original. The Zig 0.17
reference reads to actual EOF and probes one byte beyond the inclusive 512 MiB
cap, matching the original 0.15 reader. Synthetic cap−1, exact-cap and cap+1
cases verify that boundary. The independent focused follow-up accepted the
duplicate-growth and actual-EOF fixes with no confirmed findings; its
[`final-review.json`](benchmark/results/final-review.json) records the scoped
source trace and the inspected 100-case suite result. It did not run fresh
dynamic probes or review the whole port anew.
